    About this project

    A butterfly flies through a small simulated garden, pushed by wind and by its own changes of mind. The project asks a world-model question you can watch and measure: can a model imagine what happens next, and how often is it right?

    It has three modes, switched with the tabs at the top of the side panel. Landing Prediction, the original experiment, is the default. Version 1.1 adds two opt-in planning experiments.

    A compact, simulation-based proof of concept. The prediction mode uses hand-written rules and learns nothing. The two planning modes train small models on the CPU. They are inspired by, not reproductions of, the cited research.

    Landing Prediction

    One second into every flight, time pauses and the model imagines 20 possible futures from what a camera could see. It then names the flower it expects the butterfly to land on, with a probability for every flower and for “no landing within six seconds”. The prediction is frozen, the real flight continues, and the result card scores it.

    • Drag a flower or change the wind and randomness sliders: the old prediction is discarded and imagined again.
    • Keyboard: focus the garden, press N to select a flower and use the arrow keys to move it.
    • Run 500-scenario evaluation scores the model against two simple baselines on fixed seeds.

    Recorded result on 500 held-out scenarios: 72.0% top-1 accuracy, against 57.6% for the nearest-flower baseline.

    Goal-Directed Planning V1.1 · HWM-inspired

    Instead of predicting, an agent steers the butterfly to a target flower against a seeded wind. A flat planner scores steering sequences toward the flower directly. Hierarchical planners first choose an intermediate waypoint, then refine it into steering actions. Every planner gets the same budget per replan.

    • Garden: a direct route, a detour behind a hedge, or a walled-in target that cannot be reached.
    • Planner: uses either the simulator's exact dynamics or a small model learned from simulated flights.
    • Episode seed fixes the start and the wind. The same seed always gives the same episode.

    On the detour, explicit waypoints land 80% of the time against 26% for flat planning, both using exact dynamics. With the learned model, long-horizon subgoals land 60% of the time against 6% for flat planning.

    Visual Planning V1.1 · H-JEPA-inspired

    The planner sees only 50 × 30 pixel frames and a goal image, never coordinates. It plans in a representation learned from pixels. A one-level model is compared with a jointly trained two-level model whose higher level supplies subgoals.

    • Trained model picks one of three training seeds. Results differ a lot between them.
    • Add a moving visual distractor puts an independent moving object in the scene.
    • The small images show what the planner sees and the goal image it must reach.

    This is the hardest experiment, and its results are weak. The two-level model lands 33% of the time on the clean direct route on average (90%, 10% and 0% by training seed). The one-level model never lands, and neither variant solves the detour.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is this a trained neural world model?

    Not in Landing Prediction: it rolls hand-written rules forward with random sampling. The two planning modes do use small trained models, trained by an explicit command and loaded from saved files.

    Why is the prediction sometimes wrong?

    The butterfly's intent, the gusts and the random draws are hidden from the model, so its futures disagree. It is wrong in about 28% of scenarios. That is reported, not hidden.

    A planning mode says a model is missing.

    The trained models live in artifacts/. If they are absent, the mode reports it instead of guessing. Retrain them with the commands in the README.

    Does anything leave my machine?

    No. Everything runs locally in Docker, with no accounts, keys, paid APIs or cloud services.

    Why do several browser tabs show the same garden?

    The live garden is a single in-memory world shared by every connected tab. It is a demo, not a multi-user service.
